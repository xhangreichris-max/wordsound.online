How to use this list

Select a mixed set of three-, four-, and five-syllable words for a progressive difficulty ladder: students begin with the shortest and move upward, marking stress each time. The same continuum works in therapy for building length and complexity in a single session. Parents can use the mixed list for homework that requires breaking words into parts of increasing size. Poets scanning for varied line lengths gain a single source that already groups longer forms together.

What makes a word multisyllabic

Multisyllabic words contain three or more vowel sounds. The lower bound is three nuclei; the upper bound is open. The count is taken from the pronunciation dictionary, not from letter tallies.

Words people commonly miscount

Tricky cases recur across the range. “Family” and “chocolate” are often reduced below three syllables yet appear at full length. “Comfortable” and “vegetable” are frequently spoken with three nuclei while the dictionary keeps four. “University” and “opportunity” lose beats in casual talk but retain five. These reductions illustrate why a multisyllabic list generated from recorded pronunciations is more reliable than one based on spelling alone.
